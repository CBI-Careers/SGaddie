Signature Methodology

The Denial Intelligence System

Systematic ◆ Preventive ◆ Cyclical

1
Scope Account Stratification

Every review begins with a disciplined sampling methodology. Account populations are stratified by volume and a randomized selection process ensures statistical validity across the review. The scope is defined before a single account is touched.

2
Investigate Account-Level Review

Each account in the sample is reviewed at the account level. Not summary data. Not reports. The actual account. Every denial reason, every payer action, every documentation gap is examined individually to understand what actually happened.

3
Classify Root Cause Grouping

Like root causes are grouped together and ranked by the dollar amount associated with each category. This transforms individual account findings into a prioritized denial landscape that leadership can act on strategically rather than reactively.

4
Strategize Prevention Development

For each root cause category, preventive methods, recommendations, and mitigation plans are developed through research, resource consultation, and cross-functional collaboration. The goal is always prevention at the source, not recovery after the fact.

5
Charter Formal Documentation

Findings are documented in a charter defining the problem, root cause, financial impact, and recommended corrective action. The charter is reviewed with internal leadership, refined through cross-functional input, and presented to the National Denials Committee Avoidance Group for transparency and accountability.

6
Implement Mitigation Execution

Once approved, mitigation initiatives are executed across relevant workflows, systems, and teams. Implementation is tracked against the charter to ensure the corrective action is being executed as designed and that responsible parties are held accountable for adoption.

7
Monitor Continuous Surveillance

Implemented initiatives enter a monitoring phase where denial trends are tracked to confirm prevention methods are producing downward movement. After a defined monitoring period, initiatives are archived and placed under ongoing monthly surveillance. If numbers trend outward, the cycle begins again. The system never stops.

Case Study:
Mid-Surgery Procedure
Change Protocol

◆ Anonymized Client
Large Integrated Health System
Western Region
The Problem

During a denial mitigation review of outpatient surgery accounts, a pattern was identified where claims were being denied for no authorization despite valid authorizations being on file at the time of submission. The payer was receiving claims reflecting the actual procedure performed which did not match the authorized procedure and was denying on first pass regardless of authorization validity.

Root Cause

Two intersecting failures operating simultaneously. First, an internal workflow gap — no defined process required the surgical or authorization team to notify the payer when a procedure changed intraoperatively. Second, a payer behavior issue — systematic denial of valid claims at initial adjudication. Every denied account was overturned on appeal, confirming the denial was never clinically justified.

The Solution

Two parallel paths recommended simultaneously. On the internal side, a defined mid-surgery procedure change communication process requiring surgical or authorization teams to notify the payer and update the authorization before claim submission. On the payer side, a managed care escalation to establish a formal agreement for handling procedure change scenarios — modeled after a successful precedent with another major payer.

Monitoring Framework

Five metrics established to track intervention effectiveness: monthly authorization denial volume pre and post implementation, volume of denials despite valid authorization on file, appeal overturn rate to confirm payer accountability, time from procedure change to payer notification as an operational compliance metric, and monthly dollar recovery from appeals during the transition period.
